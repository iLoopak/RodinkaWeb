CHOOSING A PLANNER

The best family planner is the one your family actually uses

There is no single winner. A family with two school-age children and a grandmother who babysits needs something different from a couple who mainly want a shared calendar. This guide helps you work out what you need from an app.

DIRECT ANSWER

What is the best app for organizing family life?

The one that every adult, and children as they grow, will actually use. Before you compare apps, decide whether you mainly need a shared family calendar or a full family planner with chores, shopping and meals, whether children and grandparents should take part, and which languages and devices matter. The right app follows from that.

Six questions before you choose

  1. 1

    Who needs to take part?

    Just two adults, or children, a grandparent or a nanny too? Check whether the app supports children’s accounts and whether helpers can see only part of the plan.

  2. 2

    What takes up most of your time?

    Activities and pick-ups, shopping, cooking, chores? Choose the app that handles your most common worry well, not the one with the longest feature list.

  3. 3

    Do you need other calendars in it?

    If your work calendar lives in Google or Outlook and you want it next to the family plan, check whether and how the app connects to it.

  4. 4

    Which language and which devices?

    Will grandparents be comfortable with the language? Does the app run on every phone in the house and on a computer?

  5. 5

    What happens without signal?

    Shopping in a basement supermarket, or a task in the car. Check which parts work offline.

  6. 6

    What is free and what is paid?

    See what the free version includes, whether the paid plan is per family or per person, and its currency and billing interval.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a family calendar or a full family planner?

If you only care about dates, a shared family calendar is enough. Once chores, shopping and meals come into it, a planner saves you from switching between several apps and chats.

Isn’t a shared Google Calendar enough?

For two adults it often is. Family apps add what a general calendar lacks: children’s accounts, family chores, a shopping list, or who is taking a child where.

Should children use the family app?

From school age it helps: a child sees their own plans and chores without asking. What matters is that the app lets children see only what is meant for them.

How much does a family planner cost?

Every app in this overview has a free version. Paid plans differ in what they include, their currency and how you pay, so we compare them in each comparison.
